# Simple Investment Choices

You've built your cash cushion, handled expensive debt, and opened the right accounts. Now: what do you actually _buy_ inside those accounts?

The answer is simpler than the financial industry wants you to believe.

## The Core Idea: Index Funds

An [index fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Index_fund) is a fund that owns a little bit of everything in a market index. Instead of trying to pick winning stocks, you just own the whole market.

**Why this works:**

- Most professional fund managers **fail to beat** a simple index fund after fees. In Australia, 89% of Australian Equity General funds trailed the S&P/ASX 200 over 15 years (to 30 Jun 2026)
- You don't need to research individual companies, time the market, or make trading decisions

Some widely used index funds, with their yearly fees:

### Australian Index Funds

- **IOZ** ([iShares Core S&P/ASX 200 ETF](https://www.blackrock.com/au/products/251852/ishares-core-sp-asx-200-etf)): the 200 largest Australian companies, 0.05% (as at Oct 2026)
- **VAS** (Vanguard Australian Shares Index ETF): the 300 largest Australian companies, 0.07% (as at Oct 2026)
- **VGS** ([Vanguard MSCI Index International Shares ETF](https://www.vanguard.com.au/personal/invest-with-us/etf?portId=8212)): developed-market shares outside Australia, 0.18% (as at Oct 2026)
- **VAF** (Vanguard Australian Fixed Interest Index ETF): Australian bonds, 0.10% (as at Oct 2026)

### US-Listed Index Funds

Many Australian and NZ brokers let you buy US-listed ETFs, which are among the cheapest funds in the world:

- **VT** ([Vanguard Total World Stock ETF](https://advisors.vanguard.com/investments/products/vt/)): shares across the whole world, 0.06% (as at 27 Feb 2026)
- **VTI** ([Vanguard](https://advisors.vanguard.com/investments/products/vti/)): the whole US share market, 0.03% (as at 28 Apr 2026)
- **VXUS** ([Vanguard Total International Stock ETF](https://advisors.vanguard.com/investments/products/vxus/)): shares outside the US, 0.05% (as at 27 Feb 2026)
- **BND** ([Vanguard Total Bond Market ETF](https://advisors.vanguard.com/investments/products/bnd/)): US bonds, 0.03% (as at 28 Apr 2026)

The low fee isn't the whole cost:

- **Currency:** you buy and get dividends in US dollars, so you pay to convert, and your returns move with the exchange rate.
- **US tax forms:** your broker will ask you to fill in a W-8BEN form, so US tax on your dividends is withheld at the lower treaty rate.
- **US estate tax:** if you aren't a US citizen or resident, your estate must file a US estate tax return when your US assets are worth over **US$60,000 (as at Oct 2026)** at your death ([IRS](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/some-nonresidents-with-us-assets-must-file-estate-tax-returns)).
- **New Zealand:** above **$50,000 (as at Oct 2026)** of overseas shares, the FIF rules apply (see [Investment Accounts](/guide/3-investment-accounts)).

A fund listed on the ASX or NZX that holds the same shares avoids most of this.

## Fees: The Silent Wealth Destroyer

Every fund charges an **expense ratio (ER)** — an annual percentage fee deducted from your returns. The difference between a cheap fund and an expensive one is enormous over time.

| Fund type                                                 | Example fees (as at Oct 2026) |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Broad index ETF (e.g. IOZ, VAS, VAF, VGS)                 | 0.05-0.18%                    |
| Diversified all-in-one fund (e.g. DHHF, Simplicity Growth, VDHG) | 0.19-0.27%             |
| Actively managed fund                                     | often several times higher    |

## ETF vs Managed Fund

|                         | ETF                                                | Index managed fund            |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **How you buy**         | Like a share on the ASX or NZX, any time the market is open | Once per day at the fund's price |
| **Minimum investment**  | Price of 1 unit                                    | Varies by provider            |
| **Automatic investing** | Harder to automate (some brokers offer it)          | Easy recurring purchases      |

**Bottom line:** The differences are minor. Don't let this decision slow you down.

## Why International Diversification Matters

Australia's share market is only about **1.6%** of the world's (end of 2023, according to [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/find-a-document/reports/rep-807-evaluating-the-state-of-the-australian-public-equity-market-evidence-from-data-and-academic-literature/)). New Zealand's is smaller still. Investing _only_ at home means betting your whole future on a small slice of the world economy, and on the same economy your job depends on.

A global fund owns thousands of companies across the world in a single purchase.

## The Missing Piece: Bonds

Total stock market funds are **100% equities**. For many investors — especially those within 10-15 years of needing the money — that's too aggressive.

[Bonds](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_(finance)>) are loans you make to governments or corporations. They pay interest and are generally less volatile than stocks.

A common rule of thumb: **your bond allocation ≈ your age minus 20** (so a 40-year-old holds ~20% bonds).

## Simple Portfolio Options

From simplest to slightly more hands-on:

### Option 1: Your Super or KiwiSaver Default (Simplest)

Your retirement money is already invested. In Australia, if you don't choose, your super goes into the fund's MySuper option, which may be a **lifecycle** option ([Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/grow-your-super/super-investment-options)): more growth assets while you're young, moving to safer assets as retirement nears. In New Zealand, check which fund type your KiwiSaver is in and whether it suits how long you have until 65.

- **You do:** Check the option, check the fees, and keep contributing.

### Option 2: One Diversified Fund

A single fund holding shares (and sometimes bonds) across your home market and the world, rebalanced for you.

### Option 4: Three-Fund Portfolio

The classic [Bogleheads three-fund portfolio](https://www.bogleheads.org/wiki/Three-fund_portfolio): home shares, international shares and bonds. In Australia: **VAS** (0.07%) + **VGS** (0.18%) + **VAF** (0.10%), all as at Oct 2026.

<Callout type="tip" title="Which option should you pick?">
If you're just starting and feeling overwhelmed: **one diversified fund** (Option 2). It's genuinely good enough for an entire career of investing.

If you already have a basic understanding and want control over your share/bond mix: **shares plus bonds** (Option 3).

The difference between these options over a lifetime is small. The difference between _any_ of them and not investing is enormous.

</Callout>

<Disclosure client:load title="Rebalancing basics">
If you hold multiple funds, your portfolio drifts over time. **Rebalancing** means returning to your target allocation.

**When to rebalance:**

- **Simple approach:** Once or twice a year
- **Threshold approach:** When any asset class drifts more than 5 percentage points from target

**Tax-smart rebalancing:**

- In super or KiwiSaver: change your investment option or how new contributions are split
- In your own name: direct _new contributions_ to the underweight asset, so you don't have to sell (and, in Australia, trigger capital gains tax)

Diversified all-in-one funds rebalance automatically. This is one of their key advantages.

</Disclosure>

<Callout type="warning">
  - **Paying high fees without realizing it** — check the expense ratio of every fund you own. If
  anything is above 0.50%, investigate cheaper alternatives. - **Holding zero bonds at age 55** —
  100% stocks is valid at 25, not at 55. - **Only investing in Australian or NZ shares** — home
  country bias is natural but costly. Own the world. - **Constantly checking your portfolio** — investors who check
  daily make worse decisions than those who check quarterly. - **Trying to time the market** — time
  in the market beats timing the market. - **Buying individual stocks with core retirement savings**
  — fine as 5-10% play money, not for your core portfolio.
</Callout>
