# Next Steps

You've read the guide. Now here's what to actually _do_ — in order, this week if possible.

## The Checklist

### This week (30-60 minutes total)

- **Calculate your essential monthly expenses** — add up housing, food, utilities, insurance, transportation, minimum debt payments, healthcare. ([Cash Cushion](/guide/1-cash-cushion))

- **Open an online or bonus savings account** if you don't have one — it takes about 10 minutes. Check the conditions for the bonus rate. ([Cash Cushion](/guide/1-cash-cushion))

- **Check your super or KiwiSaver** — in Australia, check your employer is paying your super and what fees you pay. In New Zealand, check your contribution rate, and that you're on track to put in $1,042.86 a year (from 1 Jul 2025) for the full government contribution. ([Accounts](/guide/3-investment-accounts))

- **Set up one automatic transfer** — from your everyday account to savings or an investment account. Any amount. ([Before You Start](/guide/0-before-you-start))

### This month

- **List all your debts** with balances and interest rates. If anything is above 8%, make a payoff plan. ([Tackle Debt](/guide/2-tackle-debt))

- **Australia: decide on salary sacrifice.** If your income is above the 15% tax bracket (2026-27), try the [salary sacrifice calculator](/guide/3-investment-accounts) and ask your employer to set it up. ([Accounts](/guide/3-investment-accounts))

- **Pick a simple investment** — one low-cost diversified fund or a global index fund. ([Investment Choices](/guide/4-investment-choices))

- **New Zealand: check your KiwiSaver fund type** — a growth fund usually suits a long time to 65; a conservative fund usually doesn't. ([Investment Choices](/guide/4-investment-choices))

### This quarter

- **Sort out who gets your money** — your super nomination or your will (see below).

- **Write a one-page investment plan** — your target allocation, what you'll invest in, and what you'll do during a downturn (nothing). ([Staying the Course](/guide/5-staying-the-course))

- **Review your fees** — check every fund you hold, including your super or KiwiSaver. ([Investment Choices](/guide/4-investment-choices))

### Once a year

- **Review your allocation** — rebalance if needed.
- **Increase your savings rate** — by 1% every year, or by half of any raise.
- **Review your emergency fund target** — has your spending changed?
- **Check the new year's limits** — super caps change each 1 July ([ato.gov.au](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/key-superannuation-rates-and-thresholds)); NZ tax rates and KiwiSaver settings each 1 April ([ird.govt.nz](https://www.ird.govt.nz/kiwisaver))

## Decide Who Gets Your Money

This is a **short task** with enormous consequences, and it works differently in each country.

<CountryTabs>
<div slot="au">

Super isn't automatically covered by your will. You tell your fund who should get it with a death benefit nomination:

- **Binding nomination:** the fund must pay the people you name, as long as they're your dependants or your estate. Lapsing binding nominations must be renewed every 3 years.
- **Non-binding nomination:** guides the fund, but the trustee makes the final decision.
- **No nomination:** the fund decides.

</div>
<div slot="nz">

Your KiwiSaver becomes part of your estate and is paid out under your will. Keeping an up-to-date will is what matters.

</div>
</CountryTabs>

**Action items:**

1. Australia: log into your super fund and check your nomination type and who's named
2. New Zealand: make or update your will
3. Check any life insurance policies too
4. Update after marriage, separation, a new child or a death in the family, and set a yearly reminder

## When to Get Professional Help

Some situations genuinely benefit from professional advice:

**Consider a professional if you:**

- Have a complex tax situation (business ownership, stock options, real estate)
- Are going through a major life change (inheritance, divorce, early retirement)
- Have a high net worth and want tax optimization beyond the basics
- Feel overwhelmed and want someone to build a plan with you

**If you do hire someone, look for:**

- **Someone on the official register:** in Australia, the [Financial Advisers Register](https://moneysmart.gov.au/financial-advice/financial-advisers-register) (an adviser not on it can't legally give you personal advice on investments, super or life insurance). In New Zealand, the [Financial Service Providers Register](https://fsp-register.companiesoffice.govt.nz/), where advice providers must be listed.
- A **[fiduciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiduciary)** — legally required to act in your interest
- **Fee-only** — paid by you, not by commissions. New Zealand advisers must tell you about any commissions they get.
- **CFP (Certified Financial Planner)** credential

<Callout type="warning" title="Red flags in a financial advisor">

- They earn commissions on the products they recommend
- They won't clearly explain their fee structure
- They push complex products (annuities, whole life insurance) before you've maxed out simple options
- They guarantee specific returns
- They don't ask about your goals, timeline, and risk tolerance before recommending investments

</Callout>

## Keep Learning

- **[Ramit Sethi](https://www.iwillteachyoutoberich.com/)** — personal finance education focused on building a life you love. His book _I Will Teach You to Be Rich_ is the best "getting started" guide available.

- **[Morgan Housel](https://www.morganhousel.com/)** — author of _The Psychology of Money_. Essential reading for the [Staying the Course](/guide/5-staying-the-course) mindset.

- **[Bogleheads Wiki](https://www.bogleheads.org/wiki/Main_Page)** — the definitive community resource for index fund investing.

- **[John Bogle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Bogle)** — founder of Vanguard, inventor of the index fund for individual investors.

- **[Matt Levine](https://www.bloomberg.com/opinion/authors/ARbTQlRLRjE/matthew-s-levine?sref=1kJVNqnU)** — the best writer working today for understanding how financial markets actually work.

- **Warren Buffett and [Charlie Munger](https://www.amazon.com/Poor-Charlies-Almanack-Essential-Charles/dp/1953953239/)** — their consistent advice to individuals: buy a low-cost index fund and hold it forever.

The most important thing you can do is **take the first step this week** — open an account, set up an automatic transfer, buy one low-cost diversified fund. The details can be refined over time. The action can't wait.
